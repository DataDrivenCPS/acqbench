With 50 000-row batches over 257 streams (~195 rows per stream per batch) the tail pages of all 257 streams' b-tree positions fit comfortably in the 2 GB shared_buffers, so ordering the inserts does not buy cache locality — the merge is CPU-bound on per-row index insert + conflict check, not page-miss-bound. The ingest gain from old to new fix-1 (1.44× → 1.77×) is what the narrower rows and the 5× smaller unique index buy on the unchanged psycopg upsert path; Postgres is still the single ~0.9-core bottleneck with acquirium idle at 0.14 cores. fix-2's further step to 2.30× comes from moving the staging work into the acquirium process (0.75 cores) and replacing ON CONFLICT with delete-then-insert — but that is also what costs it the LIMIT pushdown and adds a ~4 ms floor to point reads.
Recommendation. PR 91 at d6b6e1a is the safe merge: it captures more of the disk win than fix-2 (5.76× vs 5.19×) and ~85% of its ingest win (1.94× vs 2.30×) with zero read-side risk. fix-2 is only worth its read-path regressions if large-scan latency matters more than latest-value reads — and if its LIMIT handling is fixed (push ORDER BY ts … LIMIT n into the Postgres SQL via postgres_query()) it would dominate on everything except the ≤ 400-row point reads, where the ~4 ms DuckDB-attach floor remains.
Comparability. Each variant ran immediately after a fresh run of the same main commit on the same day (09-02 for old fix-1, 09-04 for fix-2 and, in a later campaign, new fix-1). main's ingest and disk reproduced exactly on all three days (30.4/30.4/29.9 min, 66.47 GB), but its large-scan latencies varied 384–484 ms with host load, so raw milliseconds are not comparable across columns — the "÷ main" ratios are.
